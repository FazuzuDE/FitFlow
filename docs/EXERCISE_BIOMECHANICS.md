# CRESUM Exercise Biomechanics — Lat Pulldown v1

This is the biomechanics source of truth for the three exact Lat Pulldown variants listed below. It constrains future START/FINISH images, prompt inputs, technique descriptions, and human biomechanics QA; it does not create or approve any production asset. The general production and pair-acceptance gate remains in [EXERCISE_ASSETS.md](EXERCISE_ASSETS.md). Stable IDs and current product muscle metadata remain owned by [EXERCISE_LIBRARY.md](EXERCISE_LIBRARY.md) and the catalog.

Only a standard **seated, front-of-body cable pulldown** is specified here. Seat and thigh-pad stabilization, a high pulley, a connected cable and the same attachment throughout a pair must be visibly plausible. These are visual/biomechanical constraints, not new catalog metadata or a claim that one machine design is canonical. A comfortable, controlled range takes priority over touching the chest or reaching a fixed angle. Do not depict behind-neck pulling, body swing, forced shoulder positioning, or an anatomically impossible cable path. The original `lat-pulldown` (including legacy alias `pulldown`) remains **unspecified**: none of the three exact specifications below applies retroactively to it or its History snapshots.

The current product mapping for **each** specified variant is Primary: `lats`; Secondary: `biceps`, `upper-back`. These labels are catalog semantics, not measured recruitment, an EMG ranking, or percentages. No variant-specific muscle-mapping change is approved by this document.

## `lat-pulldown-wide-pronated-bar`

| Section                                                  | Specification                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Identity                                              | Wide Pronated Bar Lat Pulldown; exact variant of family `lat-pulldown`, distinct from the unspecified legacy record.                                                                                                                                                            |
| 2. Equipment                                             | Seated cable pulldown station with high pulley and stable seat/thigh support. Do not imply a particular selectorized or plate-loaded machine archetype.                                                                                                                         |
| 3. Attachment                                            | A bilateral wide bar capable of an overhand grip, connected to the cable at a plausible central point. Keep the same bar in both phases. Exact bar curvature and dimensions are not frozen.                                                                                     |
| 4. Grip                                                  | Pronated/overhand, hands symmetrically placed visibly wider than shoulder width without an extreme reach. No centimeter width or thumb style is prescribed.                                                                                                                     |
| 5. Setup                                                 | Athlete seated facing the cable, thighs stabilized, feet supported, torso and pelvis controlled; bar and cable overhead and in front of the body.                                                                                                                               |
| 6. START position                                        | Bar clearly above the head; arms extended overhead within comfortable shoulder motion, without forced elbow lockout or forced shoulder elevation/depression. Wrists continue the forearm line.                                                                                  |
| 7. Movement path                                         | Pull the bar down in front of the face toward the upper chest/clavicle region while the cable stays taut and tracks to its pulley. Return under control along a compatible path. No swing or large backward body drive.                                                         |
| 8. FINISH position                                       | Bar near the upper chest/clavicle region in front of the body; elbows travel below shoulder level and point generally down. Chest contact is not mandatory. Never place the bar behind the neck.                                                                                |
| 9. Range of motion / stopping criteria                   | Stop the downward phase when the bar reaches a comfortable upper-chest region or further travel would require pronounced elbow travel behind the torso, excessive torso recline, or shoulder discomfort. Return to a comfortable overhead extension without forcing end range.  |
| 10. Elbow path                                           | Both elbows move down and somewhat outward, following the wide hand placement rather than tucking tightly against the ribs; left/right paths remain reasonably symmetric.                                                                                                       |
| 11. Shoulder / scapular behavior                         | Shoulder blades move naturally with the overhead reach and controlled pull; do not pin them rigidly down/back at START or force an exaggerated shrug. Avoid an impossible or painful shoulder position.                                                                         |
| 12. Wrist / hand alignment                               | Hands firmly contact the same bar in both phases; wrists are approximately aligned with forearms, without conspicuous flexion, extension, or impossible finger placement.                                                                                                       |
| 13. Torso / pelvis                                       | Torso remains upright or modestly inclined back and reasonably stable; pelvis stays supported. Do not turn the pull into a rowing motion or depict a sudden lean-back between phases.                                                                                           |
| 14. Lower-body / support                                 | Hips remain seated, thighs under the same stabilizing pad, feet supported; no standing or kneeling version.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| 15. Cable / pulley / line of resistance                  | A taut cable connects the bar's center to the high pulley, with a coherent pull path and no cable passing through the body or disconnected/implausible pulley geometry.                                                                                                         |
| 16. Primary muscles                                      | `lats` — current product mapping, not a precise activation claim.                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 17. Secondary muscles                                    | `biceps`, `upper-back` — current product mapping, not a ranked contribution or percentage.                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| 18. Unacceptable visual / biomechanical errors           | Narrow or neutral/supinated grip; mismatched hand spacing; behind-neck bar; forced extreme shoulder abduction; bar pulled by torso swing; wrists visibly broken; asymmetrical/implausible elbows; incorrect cable connection; different bar, athlete, or station across phases. |
| 19. START/FINISH pair consistency                        | Preserve athlete/model identity, proportions, clothing, wide bar, pronated wide grip, seat/thigh-pad and pulley setup, attachment point, camera/crop, lighting, and visual style. Only movement phase materially changes.                                                       |
| 20. Open ambiguities / intentionally unspecified details | Exact grip width in centimeters, bar profile/dimensions, seat and pad settings, torso angle, scapular timing, thumb wrap, and ROM angles remain unfrozen; a future visual specification may select consistent production details without changing this exercise ID.             |

| 15. | 16. Primary muscles                                      | `lats` — current product mapping, not a precise activation claim.                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| 17. Secondary muscles                                    | `biceps`, `upper-back` — current product mapping, not a ranked contribution or percentage.                                                                                                                                                                   |
| 18. Unacceptable visual / biomechanical errors           | Straight/wide bar; palms not facing each other; hands unnaturally far apart; handle changing orientation or shape between phases; wide-flared elbow path; forced torso recline; broken wrists; impossible cable/handle connection; changed athlete or setup. |

| 20. Open ambiguities / intentionally unspecified details | Exact V-handle model and dimensions, hand spacing beyond the handle geometry, seat/pad settings, torso angle, scapular timing, thumb wrap, and ROM angles remain unfrozen.                                                                                   |

## Scope and QA handoff

### First-slice production profile boundary

The approved **CRESUM first-slice production profile** selects one consistent implementation for `lat-pulldown-close-neutral-v-handle` / Male / Technique View / START + FINISH. Its chosen generic high-pulley machine geometry (including selectorized resistance), CRESUM V-handle functional geometry, wrapped-thumb convention and modest fixed rearward torso presentation are production choices within the constraints above. They are not universal technique requirements, an approved commercial machine, or additional canonical catalog qualifiers. The profile preserves the upper-chest / upper-sternum endpoint, optional chest contact, natural scapular movement and identical V-handle orientation across the pair without prescribing exact dimensions or joint angles.

The profile and its structured inputs are maintained in `lib/exercise-assets/specifications.ts`; production workflow and limitations belong to [EXERCISE_ASSETS.md](EXERCISE_ASSETS.md). Broader biomechanics and catalog identity remain owned by this document and the existing Library/adapter respectively.

These variant-specific rejection criteria **supplement, not replace**, the general biomechanics and pair-consistency gate in [EXERCISE_ASSETS.md](EXERCISE_ASSETS.md). A future image is only a generated draft until the applicable exact-variant mechanics, pair consistency, and general visual QA are reviewed. This document does not define an asset manifest, machine model, prompt builder, generator, Anatomy/Muscle View renderer, or technique UI. A future visual specification must resolve production choices left open here and keep them consistent within each image pair without presenting those choices as new historical catalog facts.

Supporting exercise-technique references: [ACE Seated Lat Pulldown](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/158/seated-lat-pulldown/), [NSCA Basics of Strength and Conditioning Manual](https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/48a12160221541acbdc048498d77192d/basics_of_strength_and_conditioning_manual.pdf). These support conservative front-of-body, stabilized, controlled mechanics; they do not specify CRESUM's exact attachment inventory, visual style, or product muscle ranking.
